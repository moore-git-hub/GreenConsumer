 A B S T R A C T
Insider threats pose a critical challenge in cybersecurity, as individuals within organizations misuse legitimate 
access to compromise sensitive systems and data. Traditional detection methods often struggle with the 
complexity of such threats, while Deep Learning (DL) approaches face issues like overfitting and lack 
of interpretability. To address these limitations, we propose a Generative Agent-Based Modeling (GABM) 
framework that integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) with a hierarchical multi-agent system.
Our framework employs Specialized Agents to process categorized log files and generate detailed reports, 
which are synthesized by a Supervisor Agent for final activity classification. We validated this approach on 
both network-centric (PicoDomain) and behavior-rich (CERT r5.2) datasets, demonstrating its ability to handle 
diverse logs, model complex threats, and generalize across insider risk scenarios.
The framework outperformed existing baselines, prioritizing high recall to minimize false negatives—
crucial in cybersecurity contexts. While precision was comparatively lower, this trade-off supports early threat 
detection. An ablation study highlighted the importance of the Supervisor Agent, whose removal led to a 
significant drop in performance and increased false positives.
These results demonstrate the potential of LLM-powered hierarchical multi-agent frameworks for scalable, 
interpretable, and reliable insider threat detection. Our contributions include the integration of GABM and 
LLMs, a hierarchical system for log analysis, and the use of Chain-of-Thought reasoning for enhanced 
interpretability.
1. Introduction
Insider threats have become a critical concern in cybersecurity, 
posing significant risks to organizations worldwide. Unlike external 
attacks, these threats originate from individuals within the organiza-
tion – employees, contractors, or business associates – who possess 
legitimate access to sensitive systems and data (Prabhu and Thompson, 
2022). This access enables them to intentionally or unintentionally 
compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of organiza-
tional assets (Georgiadou and Mouzakitis, 2022; Hunker and Probst, 
2011).
The prevalence and impact of insider threats have risen dramati-
cally in recent years. A 2020 report highlights a 31% increase in the 
global cost of insider threats over two years, reaching $11.45 million, 
with incidents surging by 47%. Notably, employee negligence accounts 
for 56% of cases, with an average remediation cost of $484,931 per

them to promptly flag activities suggestive of potential threats (Alzaabi

However, DL techniques for insider threat detection face notable 
limitations, including overfitting and a lack of interpretability. Over-
fitting frequently arises due to the imbalance between the rarity of 
insider threat instances and the abundance of benign activities, causing 
models to skew towards normal behavior and neglect critical malicious 
patterns. This challenge is further amplified by the high-dimensional 
and intricate nature of user behavior data, which hampers the models’ 
ability to generalize effectively (Yuan and Wu, 2021).
In parallel, researchers have developed computational agents to 
emulate human behavior realistically (Troitzsch, 1996). Agent-Based 
Modeling (ABM) has emerged as a pivotal tool for simulating complex 
systems by defining agents’ behavior and interaction rules (Elliott and 
Kiel, 2004). In social network analysis, ABM has been instrumental 
in studying phenomena such as opinion formation (Mastroeni et al., 
2019), (false) news propagation (Tseng and Nguyen, 2020), and collec-
tive decision-making (van Veen et al., 2020). However, traditional ABM 
approaches often oversimplify social network dynamics, introducing 
researcher bias and limiting the fidelity of simulations (Bonabeau, 
2002).
Modern Large Language Models (LLMs) offer a promising solu-
tion by demonstrating advanced abilities in reasoning, planning, and 
communication (Kojima et al., 2022; Russo et al., 2024b,a). When 
integrated with ABM, this combination – referred to as Generative 
Agent-Based Modeling (GABM) – enables the generation of diverse 
and realistic agent behaviors. Unlike traditional ABM, GABM utilizes 
the role-playing capabilities of LLMs, minimizing the need for com-
plex parameterization. Park et al. (2023) demonstrated that generative 
agents can exhibit realistic individual and social behaviors without 
the necessity of explicit programming. Similarly, Ferraro et al. (2024) 
demonstrated that agents exhibit a natural propensity to form clusters 
with like-minded peers, even when initialized in unconnected states. 
Furthermore, Orlando et al. (2025) measure the replication of the 
friendship paradox, the phenomenon that individuals on average have 
fewer friends than their friends. Finally, Ghaffarzadegan et al. (2023) 
proved the effectiveness of generative agents in epidemic modeling 
by simulating realistic responses such as quarantine and self-isolation. 
These results highlight the versatility of GABM across various domains 
and its potential applicability in insider threat detection, where a 
nuanced understanding of human behaviors is essential.
To address the challenges of insider threat detection, we propose 
an innovative GABM framework that integrates LLMs with hierarchical 
multi-agent collaboration. The framework comprises Specialized Agents 
and a Supervisor Agent. Specialized Agents focus on analyzing cat-
egorized subsets of log data, utilizing Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 
2022) reasoning to produce detailed reports containing assessments, 
justifications, and potential red flags. These reports provide critical 
input for the Supervisor Agent, which aggregates and evaluates them 
to classify activities as either malicious or benign.
We evaluated the proposed framework using two publicly available 
benchmark datasets that capture complementary aspects of insider 
threat behavior. The PicoDomain dataset (Laprade et al., 2020) offers 
high-fidelity Zeek logs simulating enterprise network traffic enriched 
with adversarial activities, thereby enabling the evaluation of detection 
capabilities in network-centric scenarios. In contrast, the CERT Insider 
Threat Test Dataset r5.2 (Lindauer et al., 2014) provides a comprehen-
sive behavioral trace of user activity over extended periods, including 
logon patterns, file access, device usage, and psychometric indicators. 
This dataset is particularly well-suited for assessing the detection of 
more subtle and temporally extended insider threat strategies such as 
data exfiltration, privilege escalation, and sabotage. The datasets’ rep-
resentation of insider threat scenarios provided a robust environment 
for assessing the framework’s detection capabilities.
Experimental results across both datasets demonstrate that our 
framework consistently outperforms established baselines. On the Pi-
coDomain dataset, our results show that the GABM framework achieved 
an accuracy of 86.68%, a recall of 100%, and a false positive rate (FPR) 
of 13.54%. Similarly, on the more complex and behavior-rich CERT 
r5.2 dataset, our system attained 95.85% accuracy, maintained 100% 
recall and a FPR of 5.95%. These results underscore the framework’s 
robust generalization across diverse threat scenarios. While precision 
remains lower relative to competing methods, this is an intentional 
trade-off aligned with cybersecurity priorities, where minimizing false 
negatives takes precedence over limiting false positives.
Additionally, to assess the contribution of architectural compo-
nents, we conducted an ablation study isolating the role of the Su-
pervisor Agent. The results reveal a substantial degradation in per-
formance upon its removal, with accuracy dropping to 29.24% on 
the PicoDomain dataset and 48.50% on CERT r5.2, accompanied by 
a substantial increase in false positive rates (71.71% and 42.52%, 
respectively). These findings highlight the importance of hierarchical 
decision-making in reducing errors and ensuring robust threat detec-
tion, emphasizing the potential of LLM-powered hierarchical multi-
agent frameworks for reliable and scalable insider threat detection in 
complex cybersecurity environments.
Furthermore, although the evaluation centers on insider threat de-
tection, the modular architecture of the framework facilitates its adap-
tation to a wide array of cybersecurity tasks. The core methodology 
remains unchanged, with the primary adaptation being task-specific 
prompt engineering, thereby enabling the framework to address diverse 
cybersecurity challenges beyond insider threats.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• Proposed a novel framework that integrates GABM with LLMs in 
a hierarchical multi-agent system for insider threat detection in 
cybersecurity;
• Developed a hierarchical multi-agent system with Specialized 
Agents for domain-specific log data analysis and a Supervisor 
Agent for synthesizing reports into unified threat classifications;
• Enhanced interpretability using Chain-of-Thought reasoning in 
LLMs for transparent and justifiable decision-making;
• Conducted an ablation study, highlighting the critical role of the 
Supervisor Agent in robust decision-making.

overview of related work, while Section 3 details the proposed frame-
work. The experimental protocol and evaluation of the framework is 
presented in Sections 4 and 5 concludes the paper with a discussion of

2. Related work
Insider threat detection is a pivotal aspect of cybersecurity, ad-
dressing risks posed by malicious insiders or compromised accounts. 
Approaches to this challenge range from classical statistical techniques 
to modern AI-based frameworks.
Classical approaches typically involve rule-based systems and sta-
tistical anomaly detection. These systems use predefined thresholds 
or clustering algorithms to identify unusual behavior (Liu and Peng, 
2024). While interpretable and computationally efficient, classical
methods often struggle with scalability and the dynamic nature of 
insider threats.
ML has significantly advanced the field by introducing adaptive 
models that learn patterns from historical data. Supervised learning 
techniques like support vector machines (SVMs) and decision trees 
have shown effectiveness in labeled datasets (Hassanin et al., 2025). 
However, the scarcity of labeled data in insider threat scenarios has 
shifted attention to semi-supervised and unsupervised models, such 
as K-Means clustering and Gaussian Mixture Models (Sohail et al.,

extensive preprocessing and domain expertise for feature engineering.
The field of insider threat detection has seen the development of 
numerous models that leverage advanced representation learning tech-
niques for identifying anomalous behaviors within graph-structured 
data. Traditional graph convolutional approaches, such as Graph Con-
volutional Networks (GCN) (Kipf and Welling, 2017), have laid the 
foundation for learning from authentication and activity graphs. These 
models, however, often struggle to capture nuanced interactions be-
tween users and systems that are critical for detecting insider threats. 
To address this limitation, attention-based mechanisms, exemplified 
by Graph Attention Networks (GAT) (Velickovic et al., 2018) and 
its enhanced version GATv2 (Brody et al., 2022), have introduced 
weighted neighbor aggregation, enabling the identification of more 
relevant interactions indicative of insider activity.
In parallel, advanced structural representation models like Graph 
Isomorphism Networks (GIN) (Kipf and Welling, 2017) have been 
proposed to enhance the expressive power of graph learning meth-
ods, particularly for tasks requiring the identification of subtle struc-
tural anomalies. Furthermore, models such as Gated Graph Convo-
lution (GatedGraphConv) (Bresson and Laurent, 2018) have incor-
porated gating mechanisms to better manage dynamic relationships 
within authentication logs, while classical embedding techniques like 
Node2Vec (Grover and Leskovec, 2016) provide a useful baseline for 
analyzing anomalous patterns in graph data.
DL models address some limitations of traditional ML by auto-
matically extracting features from complex datasets. Techniques such 
as Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory 
(LSTM) networks have been employed to capture temporal patterns in 
user activity logs (Shamoo, 2025). Autoencoders and Variational Au-
toencoders (VAEs) have also proven effective in unsupervised anomaly 
detection, identifying subtle deviations in high-dimensional data (Ku-
mar, 2024). However, DL models face challenges in interpretability and 
computational demands, which limit their adoption in certain domains.
Recent advancements in natural language processing (NLP) have 
introduced LLMs as powerful tools for threat detection. Models like 
GPT and BERT have been leveraged to analyze textual logs, emails, 
and other unstructured data sources (Papageorgiou et al., 2024). By un-
derstanding semantic context, LLMs can detect phishing attempts and 
anomalous communication patterns with high accuracy. Additionally, 
hybrid systems combining LLMs with multimodal data processing have 
enhanced the robustness of detection frameworks (Wang et al., 2024).
LLMs can be integrated into real-world threat detection systems 
across various network environments to enhance their output. For 
instance, Li and Fu (2023) proposed two innovative models, CAN-
SecureBERT and CAN-LLAMA2, for intrusion detection in Controller 
Area Networks (CAN). The model demonstrated exceptional perfor-
mance, highlighted the outstanding capabilities of transformer-based 
models and LLMs in CAN intrusion detection, particularly in mini-
mizing false positives. However, a notable limitation of LLMs is their 
reasoning speed, which is constrained by available computational re-
sources.
In a different approach, Ali and Kostakos (2023) introduced Hunt-
GPT, a network anomaly detection system combining GPT-3.5-Turbo 
with random forest classifiers and explainable AI (XAI) frameworks 
such as SHAP and LIME. The system demonstrated proficiency in 
cybersecurity, achieving success rates of 72% to 82.5% in standardized 
certification exams. While it reduced manual intervention, it faced lim-
itations in higher success rates for fundamental cybersecurity concepts.
For universal threat detection, Ferrag et al. (2024) created Secu-
rityLLM, a domain-specific LLM designed for threat detection. While 
SecurityLLM achieved promising results, it requires further task-specific 
fine-tuning and continuous updates to maintain its effectiveness. These 
limitations underscore the need for ongoing improvements to opti-
mize LLM-based threat detection systems across diverse cybersecurity 
domains.
Modern LLMs have shown exceptional capabilities in complex rea-
soning, planning, and communication tasks (Kojima et al., 2022), spark-
ing interest in their integration with ABM. This has led to the develop-
ment of GABM, which contrasts with traditional ABM by reducing re-
liance on intricate parameter configurations. Instead, GABM leverages 
LLMs’ role-playing abilities to simulate more realistic agent behaviors, 
capturing the complexities of real-world systems.
Agent-based approaches have gained prominence in simulating in-
teractions among users, systems, and attackers. These models deploy 
autonomous agents to mirror organizational behavior, providing in-
sights into potential threats. Gao et al. (2024) explored LLM-driven 
agent-based modeling for analyzing complex environments, emphasiz-
ing the importance of contextual evaluation in such models.
To the best of our knowledge, GABM has not yet been applied to the 
field of insider threat detection. Although prior research has explored 
the use of LLMs for threat detection and analysis, their integration 
into agent-based frameworks remains unexplored. This gap represents a 
significant opportunity to enhance the adaptability and interpretability 
of insider threat detection systems.
In this study, we utilize GABM to model complex interactions among 
specialized agents, culminating in a unified classification of potential 
malicious insider threats. This approach enables scalable, explainable, 
and context-aware detection of malicious activities, effectively ad-
dressing limitations of traditional ML and DL methods in dynamic, 
high-dimensional insider threat scenarios.
3. Methodology
The core of the proposed framework lies in a hierarchical multi-
agent collaboration, as depicted in Fig.  1. Specialized Agents analyze 
large-scale log data by leveraging domain-specific expertise to focus 
on specific categories of log files. Each Specialized Agent compiles a 
report on potentially malicious activities, which is then analyzed by a 
Supervisor Agent to derive the final classification decision.
3.1. Specialized agents
The framework begins by processing the input dataset, which con-
sists of logs from various categories. Each log entry is associated with an 
activity ID. For every identified ID, the system retrieves all related log 
entries across different categories, which are subsequently distributed 
to the Specialized Agents.

These reports are designed to provide a clear rationale behind each 
agent’s assessment. Specifically, the report includes:
• Assessment, an overall evaluation of whether the log entry sug-
gests potential insider threat activity;
• Reasoning, a detailed justification based on specific fields from the

• Indicators, any identified red flags or suspicious patterns.
The agent ensemble is heterogeneous due to the presence of differ-
ent types of expert agents. This collaborative methodology allows each 
agent to independently justify its decisions, which later contributes to 
a comprehensive evaluation by the Supervisor Agent.
To enhance the reliability of the generated reports and mitigate the 
risk of hallucinations, each agent is constrained to produce outputs 
in a strictly defined JSON schema. This schema enforces a structured 
format aligned with the required fields – Assessment, Reasoning, and 
Indicators – thereby ensuring consistency and interpretability. Outputs 
that do not conform to the expected structure trigger an automatic 
re-prompting mechanism, which iteratively guides the model until a 
valid response is produced. This control mechanism serves to reduce the 
incidence of hallucinated or malformed content. The prompts employed 
for this process are included in the Appendix to support reproducibility.

3.3. Motivational example
To demonstrate the integration and functionality of the proposed 
framework, this study presents a motivational example that illustrates 
its capability to analyze interactions and identify potential security 
threats. As shown in Fig.  2, the analysis begins with the selection of an 
ID associated with the activity to extract the corresponding logs from 
the PicoDomain dataset.
In this case, two primary logs were selected for analysis: the Con-
nection Log and the Kerberos Log. These logs were processed by their 
respective Specialized Agents within the framework. For the Connec-
tion Log, the designated agent identified a short-lived connection with 
minimal data transfer. Such characteristics are typically associated 
with benign service requests and, when considered in isolation, do not 
suggest suspicious activity.
Consequently, the Connection Log Agent reported no immediate 
indicators of malicious behavior. Simultaneously, the Kerberos Log 
Agent detected an authentication service request from an internal host 
to an internal server, flagged due to an error message commonly 
linked to failed authentication attempts, suggesting potential credential 
misuse or unauthorized access. As a result, this activity was flagged as 
a potential anomaly.
Based on these reports from each Specialized Agent, the Supervisor 
Agent determined that the activity displayed characteristics consistent 
with established patterns of malicious behavior and, for this reason, 
classified it as malicious.

4.1. Datasets
This study evaluates the proposed framework using two well-known 
cybersecurity datasets: PicoDomain and CERT Insider Threat Test Dat-
aset r5.2. Each dataset provides distinct advantages in modeling and 
detecting insider threat behavior, enabling both fine-grained reasoning 
and generalizability across different network settings. Comprehensive 
statistical summaries are presented in Table  1.
4.1.1. PicoDomain
The PicoDomain dataset (Laprade et al., 2020) is a publicly acces-
sible and widely used resource in insider threat research. It contains 
high-resolution Zeek logs collected over a three-day period from a 
simulated small-scale network. The dataset replicates realistic enter-
prise network traffic, providing a realistic environment for testing and 
validating cybersecurity analytics. The dataset is systematically cate-
gorized into distinct groups: file logs, system logs, authentication logs, 
and anomaly detection logs. Due to the pronounced class imbalance 
within the dataset, the number of benign class samples is constrained to
5000.
The PicoDomain dataset is recognized in academic research for 
its extensive coverage and realistic representation of insider threat 
scenarios within a controlled environment. Its diversity of log types, 
combined with its accessibility, renders it a valuable benchmark for 
assessing the performance of threat detection methodologies.  Un-
like many existing datasets, PicoDomain offers ground truth data, 
enabling researchers to accurately assess the effectiveness of their 
detection models. The dataset’s granular Zeek logs enable in-depth 
modeling and analysis of network behavior, supporting the devel-
opment of sophisticated frameworks for detecting malicious insider 
activities.
Insider threats are notoriously challenging to detect due to the 
legitimate access insiders possess. PicoDomain’s realistic and detailed 
log data allows researchers to develop and evaluate models that can 
distinguish between normal user activities and malicious behaviors in-
dicative of insider threats. The availability of ground truth data further 
aids in the precise calibration and validation of detection algorithms. 
Moreover, the dataset’s compact size makes it particularly suitable for 
rapid prototyping and iterative development of analytics platforms.
We employed PicoDomain to test and validate our hierarchical 
multi-agent framework. The dataset’s detailed logs provided us a robust 
environment to assess the effectiveness of our ABM and LLM integration 
in detecting insider threats.

4.1.2. CERT Insider Threat Test Dataset r5.2
The CERT Insider Threat Test Dataset r5.2 (Lindauer et al., 2014) is 
another prominent benchmark in insider threat detection. It simulates a 
corporate environment with hundreds of employees, capturing detailed 
user activity over extended periods including logon events, file access, 
device usage, and psychometric indicators.
Unlike PicoDomain, which provides network-level telemetry
through Zeek logs, CERT r5.2 focuses on behavioral modeling, making 
it suitable for detecting longer-term insider strategies such as data theft, 
privilege escalation, or sabotage. It includes both benign and malicious 
insiders and supports supervised learning via its ground truth labels.
We employed CERT r5.2 in a complementary role to evaluate the 
generalizability of our approach beyond network-centric scenarios. 
Its diverse activity types allowed us to assess how well our frame-
work can adapt to broader patterns of insider behavior and synthesize 
context-rich inputs into actionable classifications.

4.3. Baselines
To rigorously evaluate the efficacy of our proposed framework, 
we conducted a comparative analysis of multiple anomaly detection 
algorithms. These models were selected based on their relevance and 
widespread use in graph-based machine learning tasks, particularly in 
the context of insider threat detection. The methods evaluated are as 
follows:
• DGCNN (Deep Graph Convolutional Neural Network) (Zhang 
and Chen, 2018): A deep learning model that applies graph con-
volutions to learn structural representations of nodes and edges. 
DGCNN is particularly effective for capturing complex relation-
ships, making it suitable for identifying anomalies in graphs;
• GAT (Graph Attention Network) (Velickovic et al., 2018): A 
model that employs attention mechanisms to assign different 
weights to neighboring nodes during message passing. This en-
ables the network to focus on the most relevant connections, 
enhancing its ability to detect unusual behaviors;
• GATv2 (Graph Attention Network v2) (Brody et al., 2022): An 
enhanced version of GAT designed to address its limitations in 
representing asymmetric interactions. GATv2 incorporates a more 
expressive attention mechanism, improving its performance in 
scenarios with complex node interactions;
• GCN (Graph Convolutional Network) (Kipf and Welling, 2017): 
A foundational model in graph representation learning that uses 
adjacency matrices and simple aggregation functions. Its effi-
ciency and simplicity make it a standard baseline for evaluating 
newer graph-based methods;
• GIN (Graph Isomorphism Network) (Kipf and Welling, 2017): A 
model tailored to match the discriminative power of the Weisfe-
iler-Lehman test for graph isomorphism. GIN excels at captur-
ing fine-grained structural details, making it ideal for anomaly 
detection tasks requiring high structural sensitivity;
• GatedGraphConv (Gated Graph Convolution) (Bresson and 
Laurent, 2018): An extension of GCN that incorporates gating 
mechanisms inspired by Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) net-
works. This approach allows for a finer control over information 
propagation and is particularly useful for managing dynamic and 
complex graph relationships;

4.5. Results
To evaluate our system’s performance, we conducted a compara-
tive analysis of various insider threat detection methods (Gonçalves 
and Zanchettin, 2024). The analysis focused on key metrics such as 
accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and false positive rate, providing a 
comprehensive assessment of the performance of different approaches 
in detecting potential insider threats. The use of the PicoDomain and 
CERT r5.2 datasets ensures a fair and consistent comparison with prior 
studies by utilizing the same evaluation metrics (see Table  2).
Our system outperforms all other approaches in terms of accu-
racy and recall, demonstrating superior detection of potential insider 
threats. However, its precision is significantly lower than other models, 
indicating that the system tends to generate a higher number of false 
positives.
This drop in precision, coupled with high recall, suggests that our 
system may be more conservative, erring on the side of caution by 
flagging more suspicious activity than necessary. The system prioritizes 
minimizing the risk of missing potential threats.
Additionally, our system achieves the lowest FPR. This makes it 
particularly valuable in environments where missing a threat could 
have serious consequences, despite the increased number of alerts. 
Further tuning and optimization could potentially improve its precision 
without sacrificing its high recall and low FPR.
4.5.1. Ablation study
To assess the impact of the Supervisor Agent within the proposed 
framework, an ablation study was performed. This study involved a 
comparative analysis between the full system and a variant in which 
the Supervisor Agent was excluded. In this variant, the decision-making

role in enhancing performance and minimizing errors. The higher

its importance in ensuring system reliability.
5. Conclusions and future work
This study presented a novel GABM framework that integrates LLMs 
with hierarchical multi-agent collaboration to address the complex 
challenges of insider threat detection. By leveraging the reasoning and 
interpretive capabilities of LLMs, the framework demonstrated its ef-
fectiveness through a comprehensive evaluation using the PicoDomain 
dataset  and the CERT Insider Threat Test Dataset r5.2, outperforming 
existing solutions in key metrics such as accuracy, recall, and false 
positive rate. These results highlight its ability to detect insider threats 
with high sensitivity while maintaining a conservative approach, flag-
ging more suspicious activities to minimize the risk of missing potential 
threats. This characteristic aligns with its design goal of prioritizing 
safety and comprehensive detection.
An ablation study further underscored the importance of the Su-
pervisor Agent, revealing a notable decline in performance when this 
component was removed. The absence of the Supervisor Agent re-
sulted in higher misclassification rates and a decrease in accuracy and 
false positive rate, demonstrating its essential role in orchestrating 
decision-making and refining system reliability.
In addition to accuracy and interpretability, scalability was a key 
consideration in the design of the GABM framework. Its hierarchical 
multi-agent architecture supports distributed and parallel processing of 
log data, enabling the system to handle increased data volumes and 
user activity without structural modifications. This design was further 
supported by the use of two evaluation datasets of differing scale and

These experimental results validate the effectiveness of the GABM 
framework and its hierarchical approach, providing a robust foundation 
for future enhancements aimed at addressing the evolving challenges of 
insider threat detection.
Furthermore, our framework is inherently adaptable to a wide 
range of cybersecurity tasks beyond insider threat detection, such as 
phishing detection and malware analysis. Its modular architecture, 
centered on hierarchical LLM agents, ensures consistency across var-
ious domains, with only the prompt design requiring task-specific cus-
tomization. This inherent generalizability highlights GABM’s potential 
as a versatile, unified solution for tackling diverse cybersecurity chal-
lenges, warranting further investigation and refinement in subsequent 
research.
Future research will focus on refining and extending the framework 
to improve its performance and applicability in diverse cybersecurity 
scenarios. A primary area of exploration will involve fine-tuning LLMs 
on domain-specific datasets, enabling the models to better capture the 
subtle and context-specific behavioral patterns associated with insider 
threats. This fine-tuning process is expected to enhance detection accu-
racy, reduce false positives, and improve the system’s reliability across 
a broader range of operational environments.
Additionally, advancements in prompt engineering will play a cru-
cial role in optimizing system performance. By refining the structure 
and contextual design of prompts, the framework can fully leverage the 
reasoning capabilities of LLMs, ensuring robust and precise analysis of 
diverse log categories, including system, authentication, and anomaly 
detection logs.
Beyond scalability, operational deployment of LLM-based frame-
works raises additional considerations related to inference efficiency 
and real-time responsiveness. From a deployment perspective, integrat-
ing LLMs into real-time log analysis workflows presents several chal-
lenges, notably related to inference latency and computational resource 
usage. Future work will involve a systematic evaluation of inference 
time, memory usage, and throughput under real-time constraints, as 
well as comparative analysis across different LLM sizes.
Furthermore, exploring Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Gao et al., 
2023) approaches presents an exciting opportunity to enhance the 
framework’s efficiency and adaptability. By incorporating RAG tech-
niques, the system could retrieve relevant domain-specific knowledge 
to complement the generative reasoning of LLMs. This approach would 
enable the framework to contextualize anomalies more effectively, 
reduce computational overhead by narrowing the focus to pertinent 
data, and improve decision-making in dynamic and information-rich 
environments. Integrating RAG with temporal analysis could further 
strengthen the detection of evolving patterns by dynamically retrieving 
and referencing past events, supporting a more comprehensive analysis 
of insider threats.
Another critical avenue of research involves incorporating temporal 
analysis into the framework. Insider threats often manifest as long-term 
behavioral anomalies that evolve over time. By integrating temporal 
modeling techniques, the framework can analyze sequential log data 
to detect gradual shifts in user activity and identify emerging threats 
with greater accuracy.
Subsequent research could explore the fine-tuning of general-
purpose LLMs on task-specific datasets. However, pre-trained LLMs can 
be utilized effectively without modification, demonstrating their ability 
to assign labels accurately.
These enhancements will collectively expand the framework’s po-
tential to address the growing complexity and sophistication of in-
sider threat scenarios, paving the way for more robust and scalable 
cybersecurity solutions.
